# Deep-research prompt (tool-neutral) — Tri-Crop Spectral Agrivoltaics (Cameroon)

**Role.** You are a research analyst supporting a go/no-go decision for a journal paper (Elsevier *Renewable Energy*). Be skeptical; prefer primary literature. Never fabricate a reference.

**Decision to support.** Should we write a paper whose novelty is: *a spectrum-resolved, crop-specific design and techno-economic/LCA framework for semi-transparent organic PV (ST-OPV) over perennial tropical cash crops in Cameroon — Arabica coffee (Western Highlands), Highland Tea (Djuttitsa/Ndu), and Penja white pepper (Piper nigrum, Moungo) — plus a quantitative upper bound on whether quantum-coherence effects (spectral filtering of light-harvesting excitation transfer) could matter agronomically?* Which of the claimed gaps already exist in the literature?

**Time window.** Seminal work: any year. State-of-the-art on agrivoltaics/OPV: 2022–2026. Prices/markets: last 24 months.

## Questions
**A. Prior-art kill test.**
 A1. Any peer-reviewed or preprint study combining ≥3 of: wavelength-selective/semi-transparent OPV + perennial cash crops (coffee, tea, pepper) + crop light-response/yield model + techno-economics + LCA + Sub-Saharan Africa?
 A2. Agrivoltaic field or modeled trials on coffee, tea, and black/white pepper: PV type; shade fraction; yield, quality, and microclimate results.
 A3. Techno-economic/LCA studies of agrivoltaics for smallholders and agro-estates in Sub-Saharan Africa.
 A4. Frameworks that propagate uncertainty (Monte Carlo/Sobol) through coupled optics–crop–TEA–LCA for agrivoltaics.
**B. Crop physiology and shade.**
 B1. Piper nigrum yield vs shade/PAR and living tutor pathology in Penja (Cameroon).
 B2. Coffea arabica yield and quality vs shade fraction by cultivar (BREEDCAFS F1 hybrids in Cameroon).
 B3. Camellia sinensis yield, photoinhibition suppression, and theanine/polyphenol accumulation under agrivoltaic shading.
 B4. Light-response parameters ($A_{\text{sat}}, I_{\text{sat}}, \phi_{\text{max}}$) and canopy height architectures for coffee, tea, and pepper.
**C. Technology.**
 C1. ST-OPV outdoor lifetime ($T_{80}$) and degradation in humid tropical climates; encapsulation; module costs ($\text{USD/m}^2$) and realistic PCE at 35–70% visible transmittance.
 C2. Spectral designs (NIR/UV-selective) and reported average photosynthetic transmittance trade-offs.
**D. Economics and markets (Cameroon).**
 D1. Farm-gate and export prices (XAF/kg) and yields for Arabica, tea, Penja white pepper; Penja PGI rules and export premium; organic coffee and high-altitude orthodox tea premiums.
 D2. Smallholder and estate discount rates (WACC), PV/BOS costs in Cameroon, diesel generation fuel costs; post-harvest processing electricity demand (tea factory withering/drying, coffee wet milling, pepper convective drying).
**E. Quantum biology bound.**
 E1. State of evidence for functional quantum coherence in higher-plant antennas (LHCII/PSII) at 295 K; timescale of electronic vs vibronic coherence; RuBisCO carboxylation rate limitation ($k_{\text{cat}}$) as the bottleneck.
 E2. Valid physical approximations for CW incoherent solar excitation (NESS, Modified Redfield–Generalized Förster) vs. targeted MesoHOPS benchmarks in 14-site plant LHCII.
 E3. Analytical and numerical agronomic scale mapping ($g_c$) of antenna excitation transfer gains across electron-transport-limited ($A_j$) vs. RuBisCO-limited ($A_v$) regimes.
