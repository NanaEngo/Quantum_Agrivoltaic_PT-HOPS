\noindent\textbf{Summary of the work}

\noindent Coherence in photosynthetic light-harvesting complexes is well documented, yet no operating device has used it as a design variable. We present the first quantum digital twin for agrivoltaics that does so: a 9-site Fenna--Matthews--Olson (FMO) complex is coupled to a nanoparticle-on-mirror (NPoM) plasmonic nanocavity inside an organic photovoltaic (OPV) stack, forming a 9$\times$9 dressed Hamiltonian propagated with numerically exact non-Markovian process tensor dynamics (PT-HOPS/SBD). Dual-band OPV spectral filtering extends FMO coherence by 50\% at room temperature; single-molecule SERS diagnostics are retained; microclimate (FAO-56), lifecycle assessment, and BB84-secured IoT telemetry close the system. The contribution to \textit{QST} is method-led: numerically exact open-quantum-system dynamics used as a design tool for a plasmonic--biological device, with the strong-coupling regime confined to a 1\% sentinel fraction so that single-molecule quantum sensing coexists with near-baseline transport (97.1\% global canopy yield).

\noindent Our main scientific contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Numerically exact non-Markovian dynamics as a design tool:} we propagate the 9-site dressed Hamiltonian with PT-HOPS/SBD ($L=8$, $K=2$, $n=100$) through a validated convergence hierarchy. The results locate an optimal NPoM mode volume ($V_{\rm mode} = 1.2\text{ nm}^3$): dual-band filtering extends FMO coherence by 50\% and transport yields by 25\% at room temperature, while the collective strong coupling ($g_{\rm eff} \sim 2000\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$) stays confined to a 1\% sentinel fraction that preserves 97.1\% of the global canopy yield.
\item \textbf{Single-molecule quantum sensing coexisting with transport:} the same strong-coupling regime that quenches local transport provides Purcell-enhanced single-molecule SERS readout (EF up to $1.6\times10^3$ across the scan), quantifying the plasmonic trade-off that quantum-engineered photonic environments must respect.
\item \textbf{System closure with quantum-secured telemetry:} FAO-56 microclimate modeling, lifecycle assessment, and room-temperature BB84 QKD ($N_{\rm raw} \approx 1200$ bits, \qty{3.7}{\ms} edge inference on ARM Cortex-M4) integrate the quantum core into a field-scale, cybersecured deployment scenario.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Why \textit{Quantum Science and Technology}?}\nopagebreak

\noindent\textit{Quantum Science and Technology} publishes cross-disciplinary advances spanning quantum photonics, quantum biology, quantum sensing, and quantum communication. This manuscript sits at their intersection: exact open-quantum-system dynamics (quantum biology) drives the design of a plasmonic nanocavity device (quantum photonics) whose strong-coupling regime doubles as a single-molecule sensor (quantum sensing), integrated with quantum-keyed telemetry (quantum communication) and validated against field-scale constraints. Few submissions traverse all four; the physics content --- engineered photonic environments, strong-coupling trade-offs, process tensor methods at production scale --- is the core of the paper, with the agricultural application as its proving ground.
