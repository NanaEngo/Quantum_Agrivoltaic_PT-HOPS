\section{Optical transfer-matrix formalism and spectral calibration}
\label{sec:sm_tmm}

Multilayer optical propagation across the semi-transparent organic photovoltaic (ST-OPV) stack---glass substrate (\qty{1.1}{\milli\meter}, incoherent) / ITO anode (\qty{140}{\nano\meter}) / \ce{ZnO} ETL (\qty{30}{\nano\meter}) / PM6:Y6 ($1{:}1.2$) photoactive layer ($d_{\mathrm{act}} \in [\num{40}, \num{140}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$) / \ce{MoO3} HTL (\qty{10}{\nano\meter}) / ultrathin \ce{Ag} electrode ($d_{\mathrm{Ag}} \in [\num{8}, \num{22}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$) / \ce{MoO3} dielectric capping layer ($d_{\mathrm{cap}} \in [\num{0}, \num{60}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$)---is evaluated using the $2\times 2$ Abeles characteristic transfer-matrix method at \qty{1}{\nano\meter} spectral resolution ($\lambda \in [\num{300}, \num{1100}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$). Unpolarized irradiance responses average transverse-electric (TE) and transverse-magnetic (TM) modes across incident angles $\theta \in [\ang{0}, \ang{80}]$:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{OPV}}^{\mathrm{beam}}(\lambda,\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\left[T_{\mathrm{TE}}(\lambda,\theta) + T_{\mathrm{TM}}(\lambda,\theta)\right], \qquad
  T_{\mathrm{OPV}}^{\mathrm{diff}}(\lambda) = 2 \int_0^{\pi/2} T_{\mathrm{OPV}}^{\mathrm{beam}}(\lambda,\theta)\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dd\theta.
  \label{eq:tmm_diff}
\end{equation}
Complex refractive indices $n(\lambda)+i\kappa(\lambda)$ use published variable-angle spectroscopic ellipsometry datasets for PM6:Y6, D18:Y6, and ITO alongside dispersive Johnson--Christy \ce{Ag} optical constants, avoiding constant-index artifacts. Across all \num{120} Pareto-swept stacks, local energy conservation satisfies $\max |R(\lambda) + T(\lambda) + A_{\mathrm{tot}}(\lambda) - 1| = \num{4.44e-16}$. An opaque reference cell ($d_{\mathrm{act}}=\qty{75}{\nano\meter}$, $d_{\mathrm{Ag}}=\qty{100}{\nano\meter}$) reproduces the experimental PM6:Y6 benchmark ($J_{\mathrm{sc}}=\qty{25.701}{\milli\ampere\per\square\centi\meter}$, $\mathrm{PCE}=\qty{15.501}{\percent}$ vs.\ \qty{26.36}{\milli\ampere\per\square\centi\meter} and \qty{16.27}{\percent} reported by Shen et~al.~\cite{Shen2025NatCommun}).

Photosynthetically active transmittance ($\mathrm{APT}$, \qtyrange{400}{700}{\nano\meter}), extended PAR ($\mathrm{ePAR}$, \qtyrange{400}{750}{\nano\meter}), light utilization efficiency ($\mathrm{LUE} = \mathrm{PCE}\times \mathrm{APT}$), and photomorphogenic red-to-far-red shift ($\Delta(\mathrm{R{:}FR})$ comparing $[\num{655}, \num{665}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$ to $[\num{725}, \num{735}]\,\unit{\nano\meter}$) follow AM1.5G photon-flux weighting (ASTM G-173-03). The 24-design Pareto frontier yields $\mathrm{LUE}_{\max}=\qty{4.424}{\percent}$ ($\mathrm{APT}\in [\qty{14.54}{\percent}, \qty{52.06}{\percent}]$), with the focal stack $\mathrm{APT44}$ ($d_{\mathrm{act}}=\qty{60}{\nano\meter}, d_{\mathrm{Ag}}=\qty{12}{\nano\meter}, d_{\mathrm{cap}}=\qty{35}{\nano\meter}$) delivering $\mathrm{APT}=\qty{44.12}{\percent}$ and $\mathrm{PCE}=\qty{9.93}{\percent}$.

\section{Microscopic 14-site LHCII exciton--vibrational model and CW NESS bounds}
\label{sec:sm_quantum}

\subsection{Three independent 14-site Hamiltonians and dipoles}
To eliminate single-Hamiltonian bias, three monomeric LHCII 14-pigment Frenkel-exciton Hamiltonians (sites Chl~$b_{601}$--$a_{614}$) are constructed directly from primary literature: H1 takes the Redfield--F\"orster pigment-site energies and couplings of Novoderezhkin et~al.~\cite{Novoderezhkin2011} paired with point-dipole transition geometry from Liu et~al.\ (PDB~1RWT); H2 adopts the electrostatic charge-density coupling site energies and Poisson--TrEsp couplings of M\"uh et~al.~\cite{Muh2010} (PDB~2BHW); and H3 implements the MMSIC-MD parameter set of Zhu, Higashi, and Saito~\cite{Zhu2024JCTC} and Kim et~al.~\cite{Kim2025SciAdv}, with a symmetric Hermitian coupling matrix ($\max |H_{ij}-H_{ji}| = \qty{0.0}{\per\centi\meter}$) led by Chl~$a_{611}$--$a_{612}$ at \qty{+129.0}{\per\centi\meter} and Chl~$a_{604}$--$b_{606}$ at \qty{+114.0}{\per\centi\meter}. Transition dipole strengths ($\mu_{\mathrm{Chl}\,a}^2 = \qty{21.0}{\debye\squared}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{Chl}\,b}^2 = \qty{14.7}{\debye\squared}$) preserve exact non-orthogonality along the crystallographic $\mathrm{N_B}$--$\mathrm{N_D}$ molecular axes (dimer 610--611 in PDB~1RWT: $\cos\theta=\num{+0.932}$, $\kappa=\num{-1.812}$), enabling partial-secular Fano interference testing. Each chlorophyll couples to a Drude--Lorentz overdamped protein bath ($\lambda_D = \qty{50.87}{\per\centi\meter}$ matching the Renger spectral density integral~\cite{Kim2025SciAdv}, bounded over $[\num{35.0}, \num{140.0}]\,\unit{\per\centi\meter}$, $\gamma_D = \qty{50.0}{\per\centi\meter}$) plus high-frequency intramolecular modes (\qtylist{340;740}{\per\centi\meter}) at $T=\qty{295}{\kelvin}$ across $N=\num{200}$ static Gaussian disorder realizations ($\mathrm{FWHM}=\qty{110}{\per\centi\meter}$).

\subsection{Continuous-wave non-equilibrium steady state vs.\ pulsed MesoHOPS}
Under continuous-wave (CW) solar illumination filtered by $T_{\mathrm{OPV}}(\lambda)$, broadband photon-pumping operators act without inter-mode temporal phase correlation~\cite{BrumerShapiro2012,Kassal2013}. Solving the Liouville steady state $\mathcal{L}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}} = 0$ compares the full Bloch--Redfield partial-secular tensor ($\eta_{\mathrm{PS}}$) against its diagonal secular Pauli counterpart ($\eta_{\mathrm{Pauli}}$) via
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\eta_{\mathrm{NESS}} = \eta_{\mathrm{PS}} - \eta_{\mathrm{Pauli}}, \qquad
  \eta = \frac{k_{\mathrm{RC}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{term}}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}})}{k_{\mathrm{RC}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{term}}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}) + k_{\mathrm{NR}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}) + k_{\mathrm{NPQ}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(P_{\mathrm{qE}}\rho_{\mathrm{ss}})},
  \label{eq:ness_eta}
\end{equation}
with open-RC trapping $k_{\mathrm{RC}} = \qty{0.005}{\per\pico\second}$ (swept over $[\num{0.0033}, \num{0.5}]\,\unit{\per\pico\second}$), nonradiative decay $k_{\mathrm{NR}} = \qty{0.25}{\per\nano\second}$, and $q_E$ photoprotective quenching $k_{\mathrm{NPQ}} \in \{\num{0}, \num{1.5}\}\,\unit{\per\nano\second}$. A 4-site exact QuTiP-BoFiN HEOM cross-check~\cite{Lambert2023} verifies $|\eta_{\mathrm{HEOM}}-\eta_{\mathrm{Pauli}}| = \num{0.00210}$ (H2) and \num{0.000106} (H3).

Separately, full non-Markovian 14-site MesoHOPS~1.8.0 trajectories ($N=48$ disorder seeds, \qty{2000}{\femto\second} propagation, max trace-norm deviation $\le \num{1.11e-15}$) confirm that coherent transient wavepacket dynamics under pulsed initial conditions deviate markedly from secular kinetics (terminal Chl~$a_{610}$--$a_{612}$ $\mathrm{MAE} = \num{0.0198}$ on H2--DL, \num{0.2017} on H3--DL, and \num{0.2200} on H3--DL+Vib$_{340}$, where \qty{340}{\per\centi\meter} vibronic dynamics reach $p_{\mathrm{term}} = \num{0.0835}$ vs.\ \num{0.4035} Pauli), yet vanish under CW incoherent sunlight ($|\Delta\eta_{\mathrm{NESS}}| \le \num{3.370e-5}$).

\section{ERA5 microclimate, tri-crop ecophysiology, and Penja white pepper parameterization}
\label{sec:sm_agro}

Hourly meteorological drivers (\qty{8760}{\hour}, 2023 UTC) are extracted from Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis~\cite{Hersbach2020} for Foumbot (\qty{1044}{\meter}, Arabica coffee), Njombe-Penja (\qty{273}{\meter}, Penja white pepper), and Djuttitsa (\qty{1400}{\meter}, highland tea). Diffuse fractions are obtained from ERA5 direct/diffuse decomposition (annual averages \num{0.365}, \num{0.461}, and \num{0.412}; wet season \numrange{0.416}{0.502}; dry season \numrange{0.260}{0.360}). Sub-canopy radiation solves 2D row-geometry shadow projection with diffuse sky view factor $F_{\mathrm{sky}}$ (generating low-PPFD shading fractions $F_{\mathrm{lp}} \in [\num{0.277}, \num{0.544}]$ under ST-OPV depending on $\mathrm{GCR}$). Coupled leaf and module temperatures resolve hourly sensible, radiative, and latent energy balances (residual $\le \qty{1.569e-5}{\watt\per\square\meter}$). Crop assimilation couples the Farquhar--von Caemmerer--Berry (FvCB) model~\cite{Farquhar1980} with Medlyn optimal stomatal conductance~\cite{Medlyn2011} and daily FAO-56 two-bucket water stress ($\mathrm{AWC}=\qty{160}{\milli\meter}$)~\cite{Allen1998}, plus calibrated irradiance photoinhibition thresholds ($I_{\mathrm{PI}} = \qty{900}{\micro\mole\per\square\meter\per\second}$ for \textit{Piper nigrum}, \qty{1200}{\micro\mole\per\square\meter\per\second} for \textit{Camellia sinensis}). Exact Shapley causal decomposition across all $2^4=16$ coalition subsets guarantees machine-precision additivity (residual $\le \qty{7.105e-15}{\percent}$).

\section{Coupled structural ($AWC\times g_b$), meteorological stress, and seasonal monsoon-open louver audits}
\label{sec:sm_structural_louver}

To quantify biophysical parameter uncertainty that enters upstream of the \num{12}-parameter Sobol QMC ensemble (\cref{main:sec:disc_limits}), \texttt{scripts/wp3b\_structural\_and\_louver\_audit.py} propagates the full \qty{8760}{\hour} coupled FvCB--Medlyn--FAO-56--LWIR engine across two non-Sobol axes without altering baseline canonical datasets: (i) a coupled \num{3}$\times$\num{3} factorial sweep of rainfed root-zone available water capacity $\mathrm{AWC}\in\{\num{120}, \num{160}, \num{220}\}\,\unit{\milli\meter}$ and leaf boundary-layer conductance $g_b\in\{\num{0.6}, \num{1.2}, \num{2.5}\}\,\unit{\mole\per\square\meter\per\second}$, and (ii) two physically consistent synthetic meteorological stress perturbations around the verified 2023 ERA5 forcing (``warm--dry'': $\Delta T_{\mathrm{air}}=\qty{+1.5}{\celsius}$, $\Delta\mathrm{RH}=\qty{-5}{pp}$, precipitation $\times\num{0.75}$, GHI $\times\num{1.05}$, $\Delta f_{\mathrm{diff}}=\num{-0.05}$; ``cool--cloudy'': $\Delta T_{\mathrm{air}}=\qty{-1.0}{\celsius}$, $\Delta\mathrm{RH}=\qty{+5}{pp}$, precipitation $\times\num{1.25}$, GHI $\times\num{0.90}$, $\Delta f_{\mathrm{diff}}=\num{+0.08}$, recomputing $e_a$ and ambient VPD via Tetens saturation). Each corner is referenced to an open-sun control ($S_0$) evaluated under identical $(\mathrm{AWC}, g_b)$ or climate forcing (\cref{tab:sm_awc_gb_stress}). Across all three crops, wind-coupled boundary-layer conductance $g_b$ dominates soil $\mathrm{AWC}$ by more than an order of magnitude: dense, calmer canopies ($g_b=\qty{0.6}{\mole\per\square\meter\per\second}$) elevate open-sun leaf temperature and render agrivoltaic cooling strongly yield-positive ($\qtyrange{+5.20}{+29.17}{\percent}$), whereas well-mixed windy canopies ($g_b=\qty{2.5}{\mole\per\square\meter\per\second}$) keep open-sun leaves near ambient air temperature and shift shaded yields mildly light-limited ($\qtyrange{-13.40}{-1.88}{\percent}$), while preserving narrow inter-technology parity ($\qtyrange{-1.14}{+2.41}{pp}$).

We also quantify the counterfactual seasonal retraction design ($S_6$, \cref{tab:sm_monsoon_louver}) suggested qualitatively for cloudy wet months in Section~\ref{main:sec:disc_mech}, wherein panels on $S_3$ (PM6:Y6 ST-OPV, $\mathrm{GCR}=\num{0.65}$) and $S_2$ (c-Si, $\mathrm{GCR}=\num{0.40}$) retract edge-on to full open sky from June through October ($\mathrm{GCR}_h=0$, $\tau_{\mathrm{PAR},h}=1$) while remaining closed November--May. Re-evaluating the coupled \qty{8760}{\hour} crop engine, factory/BESS dispatch, \qty{20}{\yr} DCF economics, and dual-FU ISO~14040 LCA shows that although June--October retraction rescues monsoon photosynthesis by \qtyrange{+1.22}{+7.38}{pp} ($S_3$) and \qtyrange{+0.98}{+6.98}{pp} ($S_2$), the associated \qtyrange{38.0}{40.1}{\percent} loss in annual electricity revenue reduces \qty{20}{\yr} NPV by \qtyrange{349596}{784209}{\USD\per\hectare} relative to fixed arrays (and by \qtyrange{357696}{826855}{\USD\per\hectare} relative to $S_5$ dry-season drip irrigation), yielding zero (\qty{0.00}{\USD\per\square\meter}) capital headroom for motorized louver actuation wherever grid-connected power monetization anchors bankability.
