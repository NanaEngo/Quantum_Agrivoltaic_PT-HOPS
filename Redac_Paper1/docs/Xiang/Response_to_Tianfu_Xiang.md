## 1. Construction of the SBD bundles

The three production bundles per site (and the six-bundle reference) were **not** obtained by manual frequency-range selection, by a moment-based optimization, or by a stochastic projection. They were generated by a **deterministic, coupling-weighted one-dimensional K-means clustering (Lloyd's algorithm)** applied to the exponential correlation terms of the bath.

Regarding your count: in our production representation each site is coupled to **15 exponential terms** — 3 from the Drude–Lorentz Padé decomposition (K=2) and 12 from the underdamped modes. Each underdamped mode is represented by a *single* complex-frequency exponential $(g,\omega) = (\lambda_m,\ \Omega_m + i\gamma)$. This is the actual production count (the "27 exponential terms in the standard MesoHOPS representation" corresponds to the two-exponential-per-mode Brownian-oscillator form, which our installed MesoHOPS v1.7 does not support on the code path we use). The three production bundles per site are then **15 → 3**, matching the compressible hierarchy size. The bundling routine:

1. collects the 15 (g, \omega) pairs associated with each site;
2. clusters them into M groups by frequency proximity, weighted by |g|;
3. assigns to each bundle a single effective pair (g_eff, \omega_eff), where \omega_eff is the |g|-weighted mean frequency of its members and g_eff = \sum|g| over members.

The resulting effective bundles for one site, at \lambda_D = 35 cm^{-1}, \gamma_D = 50 cm^{-1}, T = 295 K, K = 2, damping \gamma = 10 cm^{-1}, are:

| 1 | 1153.29 | 1283.21 | DL Matsubara ν₁ (1288.27, 1115.77); 720 (7.2); 1050 (8.4); 1185 (5.925); 1220 (6.1); 1350 (5.4); 1500 (4.5) |

The full numerical data (15 raw exponentials, memberships, and the six-bundle reference set) are provided in `SBD_bundle_definition_site1.yaml`. The dominant bundle 0 is essentially the DL main pole (|g| ≈ 14402) carrying the six low-frequency modes; bundles 1 and 2 are the two Matsubara terms plus the high-frequency modes. The effective couplings are *sums of magnitudes*; the wavenumber convention for the Matsubara frequencies is MesoHOPS's $\omega_k = 2\pi k k_B T/\hbar c$ (i.e. \nu_1 = 1288 cm^{-1}).

The procedure is deterministic for a given mode list; no mixing/projection matrix is used and no bundle is redrawn between stochastic trajectories. The word "stochastic" in SBD reflects the methodology's motivation (compressing many bath modes into a small number of effective dissipators) rather than any stochastic element in our production construction.

## 2. Representation of a bundle

A bundle retains a record of its member modes, but in the propagated hierarchy **each bundle is represented by exactly one effective (g, \omega) pair** — a single coefficient–frequency pair per bundle, not a superposition of its internal exponentials. Consequently, one bundle corresponds to one hierarchy index (one hierarchy direction per site per bundle), even though it aggregates multiple bath exponentials. With three bundles per site and seven sites, the hierarchy carries 21 effective modes, matching the count reported in the SI.

This answers your question about Eq. (S7): the partition is applied at the level of the exponential (Padé/Brownian) decomposition — i.e. to C(t) as a sum of exponentials — and the reduced single-pair bundles are then fed into the standard MesoHOPS `GW_SYSBATH` / `L_HIER` interface. The system space remains the seven-site electronic space throughout propagation.

## 3. Electronic Hamiltonian and underdamped modes

Your reading is correct. The propagated system is the seven-site electronic space; the Drude–Lorentz contribution and the 12 underdamped modes enter exclusively through the spectral density and the system–bath correlation functions. The system–bath coupling operators are diagonal on the seven site indices, and no enlarged electron–vibrational product basis is used in the production propagation. The vibronic-basis discussion in Section S7 is an interpretive analysis of the dressed dynamics rather than a separate enlarged Hamiltonian used for propagation.

## 4. Damping constants of the underdamped modes

You are right that the damping constants were not reported in Table S3. We used a single, identical damping parameter for all 12 modes: \gamma = 10 cm^{-1}.

In our production representation the 12 underdamped modes enter the hierarchy as single complex-frequency exponentials $(g,\omega)=(\lambda_m,\ \Omega_m+i\gamma)$, where $\gamma$ is the mode damping (FWHM of the spectral peak in the Brownian-oscillator convention), $\Omega$ the mode frequency, and $\lambda_m = S\cdot\Omega$ the mode reorganization energy. This is the default fallback in our `hops_simulator` for MesoHOPS v1.7 (which does not support the `bcf_convert_sdl_to_exp` two-exponential converter we try first). All 12 modes share the same \gamma = 10 cm^{-1}.

## 5. MesoHOPS implementation

We used the public MesoHOPS v1.7 package as the propagation engine, **unmodified internally**, but with a custom wrapper: a subclass of `HopsTrajectory` that intercepts the `GW_SYSBATH`/`L_HIER` parameters before trajectory construction, applies the K-means bundling described in point 1, and then delegates to the standard MesoHOPS hierarchy and integrator. The bundling and wrapper are self-contained (two small files, ~550 lines total), and we are happy to share them.

One clarification: the production calculations were run in the standard HOPS mode of MesoHOPS together with the SBD compression. The "PT-HOPS" label in the paper refers to the process-tensor hierarchy framework that motivated the pipeline; in the production runs the process-tensor noise layer was not activated. This distinction is not stated in the manuscript.

## Correction to the Supporting Information

You found that when each bundle is represented by one conventional MesoHOPS exponential, **no** three-bundle assignment of the 15 bath contributions reproduces the target correlation function within the reported 1% criterion, with the best relative L_2 error ≈ 69%.

This is consistent with our implementation, and it means that a sentence in Section S1.4 of the SI is **incorrect**. The SI states that the bundling preserves the first two moments of the spectral density within 0.5% and the maximum relative error in C(t) below 1% for t < 1000 fs. That claim was **not verified** for the production K-means/single-pair reduction and, as your calculation shows, cannot be achieved by a single-exponential-per-bundle representation of this bath. The 69% L_2 you obtain is the correct order of magnitude for this reduction, and your difficulty reproducing this is expected rather than a misunderstanding on your part. Note also that our bundling is non-contiguous (coupling-weighted K-means in the complex-frequency plane), so an exhaustive scan of *contiguous* partitions would not encounter our assignment even before the fidelity issue. The SI Section S1.4 is corrected in the accompanying `SI_JPCL_26-06-20_CORRECTED.tex`, which replaces the fidelity claim with a description of the construction and the hierarchy-size reduction it buys at L=8.
