\opening{Dear Prof. Scholes,}

We submit the revised version of our manuscript ``Selective Vibronic Excitation for Coherent Energy Transport in Photosynthetic and Agrivoltaic Systems'' (Manuscript ID: jz-2026-00994t) for consideration as a Letter in \textit{The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters}.

We appreciate the reviewers' constructive evaluation, which has significantly strengthened the manuscript. The revised version incorporates critical updates addressing concerns regarding numerical convergence, biological realism, and clarity:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Comprehensive technical audit.} We have executed a final production audit at hierarchy depth $L=8$ and $K=2$ Matsubara terms, confirming numerical convergence within an MAE of $\approx \num{3.32 e-5}$ relative to the $L=9$ limit. This configuration was strictly enforced via a new OOM-hardened resource-aware architecture that gates parallel execution based on real-time RAM monitoring. Our audit confirms bit-perfect identical excitonic populations (numerical delta = 0.0) across multiple code refactoring cycles, resolving previous numerical inconsistencies noted by Reviewers 1 and 3.

\item \textbf{Physiological realism.} We have updated our simulation framework to incorporate the 12-mode spectral density for the FMO complex derived by Kleinekathöfer and Coker, addressing Reviewer 2's request for higher modeling fidelity.

\item \textbf{Visualization overhaul.} We have replotted all figures with a new \SI{600}{dpi} publication theme, ensuring legible axes, units, and clear comparisons as requested by Reviewer 1 and 3.

\item \textbf{Theoretical reframing.} We have clarified the mechanism of ``spectral bath engineering'' using the polaron-frame formalism, distinguishing it from trivial state preparation.

\item \textbf{Updated title.} We have revised the title to ``Selective Vibronic Excitation for Coherent Energy Transport in Photosynthetic and Agrivoltaic Systems'' to more accurately reflect the physical mechanism and the targeted application, addressing the reviewers' concerns regarding the original terminology.
\end{enumerate}

These updates reinforce our finding that selective vibronic excitation of the incident field can extend excitonic coherence lifetimes by \SIrange{20}{50}{\percent} and enhance efficiency by \SI{20}{\percent} at room temperature. We demonstrate that this approach provides the physical foundation for \textbf{symbiotic agrivoltaic systems}, where spectrally selective materials optimize both power conversion and agricultural yield. We believe these revisions fully address the reviewers' concerns and meet the standards of \textit{The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters}.
